I am a platform engineer in Berlin. I lead platform engineering and architecture for a team that builds agentic AI products, and I have been shipping software since 2016.

## How I got here

I started in 2016 as a software developer in Kochi, India. My first years were spent on billing, special offers and analytics for large energy and telecom companies in the US and the Middle East. It was not glamorous work, but it taught me what production means. One year I led a small team that cleared 40% of a ticket backlog that had been piling up for a year, and stopped a revenue leak that mattered to the client. I still think about that one when people say maintenance is boring.

In 2020 I moved to Germany for my master's. Alongside my studies I worked in applied research: first on a real time classroom platform, where I owned the end to end tests for many users clicking at once, then on an MLOps platform for my thesis, so every machine learning project in the institute could share one model lifecycle instead of building its own.

After that I spent two years building generative AI services for a large university: text, images and, the part I am proudest of, AI video lectures. We rebuilt the video pipeline so that a run went from a full day to under half an hour.

Since late 2024 I have been on a young AI team, where I joined as one of the first engineers and became part of the leadership team. My days are a mix of architecture, platform work, design reviews, hiring and helping people grow.

## What I care about

I like the parts of engineering that nobody notices when they work. A pipeline that stops a broken release. A platform where a new service starts with tests, monitoring and security already in place. An architecture decision written down well enough that someone can disagree with it a year later.

Agents make all of this more interesting. When software can choose which tool to call, questions like "who is allowed to do this" and "did the behaviour change" stop being theory. A lot of my recent open source work, such as toolwall and agent-replay, comes from exactly those two questions.

## How I learn

I learn by reading other people's code. When I want to understand something, I clone a mature open source project and read how it handles plugins, migrations or tests. Lately that has been n8n, Backstage, Hugo, Strapi, LeRobot and a handful of agent frameworks.

I also learn by building with a deadline. Last weekend I was at a robotics hackathon in Berlin, where our team taught a robot arm to tidy a desk by voice. I will write about that next.

## What to expect here

Short, practical posts about platform engineering, agentic architecture, testing AI systems and whatever I am currently taking apart. If something here is wrong or you see it differently, I would like to hear it: thirumal1206@gmail.com.
